Understanding the Rate of Change

Derivatives lie at the very heart of calculus. They measure how a function changes as its input changes, allowing us to find instantaneous speeds, slopes, and optimize complex real-world systems.

functions
Core Definition
f'(x) = limh→0 f(x + h) - f(x)h

The slope of the secant line approaches the tangent line as interval h shrinks to zero.

lightbulb Key Takeaways

  • check_circle Geometrically: The derivative is precisely the slope of the tangent line to the curve at any given point.
  • check_circle Physically: If position is a function of time, its derivative represents instantaneous velocity.
  • check_circle Notational flexibility: Denoted interchangeably as $f'(x)$, $\frac{dy}{dx}$, or $\dot{y}$ depending on context.

Why Limits Matter

Without limits, calculating slopes of curved paths would lead to division by zero ($0/0$), which is undefined. The limit operation lets us get infinitely close to zero without actually reaching it, unlocking exact precision in motion and change.
