## 1. What is a Journal?

In financial accounting, the **Journal** is the primary book where all business transactions are recorded in **chronological order** (date-wise) as they occur.

- **Origin**: Derived from the French word *"Jour"* meaning "a day" — hence, a daily record.
- **Also known as**: The **Book of Original Entry** or **Prime Entry**.
- **Process of recording**: Known as **Journalizing**.
- **Recorded unit**: Known as a **Journal Entry**.

---

## 2. The Core Foundation: Double Entry System

Accounting operates on the principle that **every business transaction has two equal and opposite effects**:
- One account is **Debited (Dr.)**
- Another account is **Credited (Cr.)**

**Total Debits = Total Credits**

---

## 3. Rules of Debit & Credit

You can determine Debits and Credits using either of the two standard frameworks:

### Method A: Traditional Approach (The 3 Golden Rules)

| Account Type | Definition & Examples | Golden Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Personal Account** | Relates to persons, firms, companies (e.g., Ram A/c, XYZ Ltd, Bank A/c, Capital A/c, Drawings A/c) | **Debit the Receiver**<br>**Credit the Giver** |
| **2. Real Account** | Relates to tangible & intangible assets/property (e.g., Cash, Machinery, Furniture, Building, Patents) | **Debit what comes in**<br>**Credit what goes out** |
| **3. Nominal Account** | Relates to expenses, losses, incomes, gains (e.g., Rent A/c, Salaries A/c, Commission Received, Interest) | **Debit all expenses & losses**<br>**Credit all incomes & gains** |

---

### Method B: Modern (American) Approach

Classifies all accounts into 5 categories: **Assets, Expenses, Liabilities, Capital, Revenue**.

| Account Category | Normal Balance | To Increase (+) | To Decrease (-) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Assets** (Cash, Stock, Furniture) | Debit | **Debit (Dr.)** | **Credit (Cr.)** |
| **Expenses / Losses** (Rent, Wages) | Debit | **Debit (Dr.)** | **Credit (Cr.)** |
| **Liabilities** (Creditors, Loan) | Credit | **Credit (Cr.)** | **Debit (Dr.)** |
| **Capital / Equity** (Owner's Capital) | Credit | **Credit (Cr.)** | **Debit (Dr.)** |
| **Revenue / Gains** (Sales, Commission) | Credit | **Credit (Cr.)** | **Debit (Dr.)** |

> **💡 Memory Mnemonic**:
> - **D.E.A.L.** (*Drawings, Expenses, Assets, Losses*) → Increase is **DEBIT**.
> - **C.L.E.A.R.** (*Capital, Liabilities, Equity, Assets Decrease, Revenue*) → Increase is **CREDIT**.

## 4. Standard Format of a Journal

A standard journal has **5 columns**:

```
+------------+------------------------------------------+--------+------------------+-------------------+
|    Date    |               Particulars                |  L.F.  |    Debit (₹)     |    Credit (₹)     |
+------------+------------------------------------------+--------+------------------+-------------------+
| 2026       |                                          |        |                  |                   |
| Jan 01     | [Account to be Debited]              Dr. |        |      10,000      |                   |
|            |    To [Account to be Credited]           |        |                  |      10,000       |
|            | (Being brief narration of transaction)   |        |                  |                   |
+------------+------------------------------------------+--------+------------------+-------------------+
```

### Components of the Format:
1. **Date**: Year at the top, followed by month and day.
2. **Particulars**:
   - The line for the debited account ends with `Dr.` against the margin.
   - The line for the credited account starts with an indent and the word `To`.
   - **Narration**: A short explanation starting with `(Being ...)` enclosed in brackets.
3. **L.F. (Ledger Folio)**: Page number in the ledger book where this account is posted.
4. **Debit (₹)**: Amount debited.
5. **Credit (₹)**: Amount credited.

---

## 5. The 4-Step Journalizing Algorithm

When analyzing any transaction, follow these 4 steps:

```text
[1. Identify Accounts] ──► [2. Classify Nature] ──► [3. Apply Rule] ──► [4. Write Entry & Narration]
```

1. **Step 1: Identify the two or more accounts involved** (e.g., Cash & Capital).
2. **Step 2: Classify each account** (e.g., Real, Personal, Nominal OR Asset, Liability, etc.).
3. **Step 3: Determine increase/decrease or flow** (What comes in? Who is the receiver?).
4. **Step 4: Record Debit first, Credit second, and write the Narration**.

## 6. Detailed Step-by-Step Solved Case Study

Let's journalize transactions for **Mr. Aryan's Enterprise** for January 2026.

---

### Transaction 1: Commenced business with Cash ₹1,00,000 (Jan 1)
- **Step 1 (Accounts)**: `Cash A/c` and `Capital A/c`
- **Step 2 (Classification)**:
  - `Cash`: Real Account (Asset)
  - `Capital`: Personal Account (Owner's representative)
- **Step 3 (Rule)**:
  - Cash comes in → **Debit**
  - Owner (Aryan) is the giver → **Credit**
- **Entry**:
  ```text
  Cash A/c ..................................... Dr.   ₹1,00,000
      To Capital A/c                                                 ₹1,00,000
  (Being business started with cash)
  ```

---

### Transaction 2: Opened a Bank Account with SBI by depositing ₹40,000 (Jan 3)
- **Accounts**: `Bank A/c` and `Cash A/c`
- **Rule**:
  - Bank (Personal) is the receiver → **Debit**
  - Cash (Real) goes out → **Credit**
- **Entry**:
  ```text
  Bank A/c ..................................... Dr.     ₹40,000
      To Cash A/c                                                   ₹40,000
  (Being bank account opened with cash)
  ```

---

### Transaction 3: Purchased goods for cash ₹20,000 (Jan 5)
- **Accounts**: `Purchases A/c` and `Cash A/c`
  *(Note: For buying trading goods, we use "Purchases A/c", not "Goods A/c")*
- **Rule**:
  - Purchases (Nominal - Expense) → **Debit**
  - Cash (Real - goes out) → **Credit**
- **Entry**:
  ```text
  Purchases A/c ................................ Dr.     ₹20,000
      To Cash A/c                                                   ₹20,000
  (Being goods purchased for cash)
  ```

---

### Transaction 4: Purchased goods from Rohit on credit ₹30,000 (Jan 8)
- **Accounts**: `Purchases A/c` and `Rohit A/c` (Creditor)
- **Rule**:
  - Purchases (Expense increases) → **Debit**
  - Rohit (Personal - Giver / Liability increases) → **Credit**
- **Entry**:
  ```text
  Purchases A/c ................................ Dr.     ₹30,000
      To Rohit's A/c                                                ₹30,000
  (Being goods purchased on credit from Rohit)
  ```

---

### Transaction 5: Sold goods for cash ₹15,000 (Jan 10)
- **Accounts**: `Cash A/c` and `Sales A/c`
- **Rule**:
  - Cash (Asset comes in) → **Debit**
  - Sales (Nominal - Revenue / Income) → **Credit**
- **Entry**:
  ```text
  Cash A/c ..................................... Dr.     ₹15,000
      To Sales A/c                                                  ₹15,000
  (Being goods sold for cash)
  ```

---

### Transaction 6: Sold goods to Priya on credit ₹25,000 (Jan 12)
- **Accounts**: `Priya A/c` (Debtor) and `Sales A/c`
- **Rule**:
  - Priya (Personal - Receiver / Asset increases) → **Debit**
  - Sales (Revenue) → **Credit**
- **Entry**:
  ```text
  Priya's A/c .................................. Dr.     ₹25,000
      To Sales A/c                                                  ₹25,000
  (Being goods sold to Priya on credit)
  ```

---

### Transaction 7: Paid to Rohit ₹29,000 in full settlement of his account of ₹30,000 (Jan 16)
- **Concept**: Rohit was owed ₹30,000. Paying ₹29,000 settles it → ₹1,000 is **Discount Received** (Gain/Income).
- **Accounts**:
  1. `Rohit's A/c`: ₹30,000 (Debit - Liability reduced fully)
  2. `Cash A/c`: ₹29,000 (Credit - Cash paid)
  3. `Discount Received A/c`: ₹1,000 (Credit - Income/Gain)
- **Entry (Compound Entry)**:
  ```text
  Rohit's A/c .................................. Dr.     ₹30,000
      To Cash A/c                                                   ₹29,000
      To Discount Received A/c                                       ₹1,000
  (Being cash paid to Rohit and discount received in full settlement)
  ```

---

### Transaction 8: Received from Priya ₹24,200 and allowed her discount ₹800 in full settlement (Jan 20)
- **Accounts**:
  1. `Cash A/c`: ₹24,200 (Debit - Cash received)
  2. `Discount Allowed A/c`: ₹800 (Debit - Expense/Loss)
  3. `Priya's A/c`: ₹25,000 (Credit - Debtor cancelled)
- **Entry**:
  ```text
  Cash A/c ..................................... Dr.     ₹24,200
  Discount Allowed A/c ......................... Dr.       ₹800
      To Priya's A/c                                                ₹25,000
  (Being cash received from Priya and discount allowed)
  ```

---

### Transaction 9: Owner withdrew Cash ₹5,000 and Goods ₹3,000 for personal use (Jan 25)
- **Concept**: Personal use by owner = **Drawings A/c**. Goods withdrawn reduce purchases at cost.
- **Accounts**:
  - `Drawings A/c`: ₹8,000 (Debit)
  - `Cash A/c`: ₹5,000 (Credit)
  - `Purchases A/c`: ₹3,000 (Credit)
- **Entry**:
  ```text
  Drawings A/c ................................. Dr.      ₹8,000
      To Cash A/c                                                    ₹5,000
      To Purchases A/c                                               ₹3,000
  (Being cash and goods withdrawn for personal use)
  ```

---

### Transaction 10: Paid Rent ₹4,000 and Salaries ₹12,000 by cheque (Jan 31)
- **Entry (Compound Entry)**:
  ```text
  Rent A/c ..................................... Dr.      ₹4,000
  Salaries A/c ................................. Dr.     ₹12,000
      To Bank A/c                                                   ₹16,000
  (Being rent and salaries paid via cheque)
  ```

## 8. Special & Critical Transactions Guide

### 1. Trade Discount vs. Cash Discount

| Feature | Trade Discount | Cash Discount |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Purpose** | To promote bulk buying | To encourage prompt/early payment |
| **Recording in Journal** | **Never recorded separately** in the Journal. Deducted directly from invoice price. | **Explicitly recorded** (`Discount Allowed A/c` or `Discount Received A/c`). |

**Example**: Bought goods of list price ₹10,000 at 10% Trade Discount and 2% Cash Discount for cash immediately.
- List Price = ₹10,000
- Less: 10% Trade Discount = ₹1,000 → Actual Purchase Price = ₹9,000
- Cash Discount = 2% of ₹9,000 = ₹180
- Net Cash Paid = ₹9,000 - ₹180 = ₹8,820

**Entry**:
```text
Purchases A/c ................................ Dr.      ₹9,000
    To Cash A/c                                                    ₹8,820
    To Discount Received A/c                                         ₹180
(Being goods purchased with 10% trade discount and 2% cash discount)
```

---

### 2. Bad Debts & Recovery

- **When a customer becomes insolvent and cannot pay**:
  ```text
  Bad Debts A/c (Loss) ......................... Dr.      ₹X,XXX
      To Debtor's A/c                                                ₹X,XXX
  ```
- **When previously written-off bad debt is recovered later**:
  ```text
  Cash / Bank A/c .............................. Dr.      ₹X,XXX
      To Bad Debts Recovered A/c (Gain)                              ₹X,XXX
  ```

---

### 3. Depreciation on Fixed Assets
- Non-cash expense reflecting wear and tear:
  ```text
  Depreciation A/c (Expense) ................... Dr.      ₹X,XXX
      To Asset A/c (e.g., Machinery A/c)                             ₹X,XXX
  ```

### 📝 Practice Transaction 1: Credit Purchase with Cash Cartage
> **Transaction**: Purchased goods from Bharat on credit for ₹45,000 and paid ₹1,200 for cartage/carriage in cash.

* **💡 Hint 1 (Goods on Credit)**: Bharat gave goods without taking cash right away. What is his account type (Personal/Creditor)?
* **💡 Hint 2 (Cartage Expense)**: Cartage is a transportation expense paid immediately in cash. Should it be recorded separately?

<details>
<summary><b>👉 Click to View Full Step-by-Step Solution</b></summary>

- **Accounts**:
  1. `Purchases A/c` (Nominal - Expense)
  2. `Cartage / Carriage Inward A/c` (Nominal - Expense)
  3. `Bharat's A/c` (Personal - Giver / Creditor)
  4. `Cash A/c` (Real - Goes out)
- **Rule**:
  - Purchases (Expense increases) → **Debit ₹45,000**
  - Cartage (Expense increases) → **Debit ₹1,200**
  - Bharat (Giver / Liability increases) → **Credit ₹45,000**
  - Cash (Asset goes out) → **Credit ₹1,200**
- **Entry**:
  ```text
  Purchases A/c ................................ Dr.     ₹45,000
  Cartage A/c .................................. Dr.      ₹1,200
      To Bharat's A/c                                               ₹45,000
      To Cash A/c                                                    ₹1,200
  ```
