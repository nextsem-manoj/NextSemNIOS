## Section 1: Meaning, Scope & Objectives of Accounting

### Q1. Which of the following represents the correct sequential order of the accounting cycle?
- **A)** Recording → Classifying → Summarizing → Identifying → Interpreting
- **B)** Identifying → Measuring → Recording → Classifying → Summarizing → Communicating
- **C)** Classifying → Recording → Summarizing → Communicating → Analyzing
- **D)** Measuring → Summarizing → Recording → Classifying → Interpreting

> **Answer:** **B) Identifying → Measuring → Recording → Classifying → Summarizing → Communicating**  
> **Explanation:** Accounting begins by identifying financial transactions from source documents, measuring them in monetary terms, recording them chronologically in the Journal, classifying them by posting to Ledger accounts, summarizing them into Trial Balance and Financial Statements, and finally communicating the results to stakeholders.

---

### Q2. Bookkeeping is primarily concerned with which stage of accounting?
- **A)** Analyzing and interpreting financial data for management decisions
- **B)** Designing internal control systems and auditing records
- **C)** Identifying financial transactions, measuring in money, and recording in journal and ledger
- **D)** Forecasting future cash flows and budgeting

> **Answer:** **C) Identifying financial transactions, measuring in money, and recording in journal and ledger**  
> **Explanation:** Bookkeeping is the primary and routine clerical stage of accounting. It deals with recording transactions in original entry books and posting them into ledgers. Accounting begins where bookkeeping ends and involves summarizing, analyzing, and communicating financial performance.

---

### Q3. Which of the following is considered an **external user** of financial accounting information?
- **A)** Chief Executive Officer (CEO)
- **B)** Production Manager
- **C)** Commercial Banks & Lenders
- **D)** Internal Auditor

> **Answer:** **C) Commercial Banks & Lenders**  
> **Explanation:** External users are outside the business entity who need financial statements to make investment or lending decisions (e.g., banks, tax authorities, investors, suppliers, and regulatory bodies). CEOs, managers, and internal auditors are internal users.

---

### Q4. Which of the following is an inherent **limitation** of financial accounting?
- **A)** It maintains systematic records of business transactions
- **B)** It facilitates preparation of final financial statements
- **C)** Non-monetary qualitative factors (e.g., employee morale, management skill) are ignored
- **D)** It assists in complying with tax regulations

> **Answer:** **C) Non-monetary qualitative factors (e.g., employee morale, management skill) are ignored**  
> **Explanation:** According to the Money Measurement Concept, accounting only records events capable of being measured in terms of money. Vital qualitative elements—such as staff dedication, strike threats, or management expertise—cannot be captured on balance sheets.

---

### Q5. "Accountancy" is best described as:
- **A)** The mechanical process of writing in day books
- **B)** The systematic body of knowledge, principles, and techniques governing accounting practice
- **C)** The preparation of income tax returns only
- **D)** Balancing the cash book at the end of each day

> **Answer:** **B) The systematic body of knowledge, principles, and techniques governing accounting practice**  
> **Explanation:** While bookkeeping is the recording process and accounting is the practical application, Accountancy is the overarching academic discipline and framework of rules, concepts, and principles that dictate how accounting is conducted.

## Section 2: Basic Accounting Terms & Definitions

### Q6. The excess of total assets over total external liabilities represents:
- **A)** Net Sales
- **B)** Capital (Owner's Equity)
- **C)** Gross Profit
- **D)** Reserve Surplus only

> **Answer:** **B) Capital (Owner's Equity)**  
> **Explanation:** From the fundamental accounting equation: $\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Capital}$. Therefore, $\text{Capital} = \text{Assets} - \text{Liabilities}$. Capital represents the proprietor's residual claim on the assets of the business.

---

### Q7. Goods withdrawn by the proprietor from the business for personal household use are termed as:
- **A)** Business Expenses
- **B)** Drawings
- **C)** Capital introduced
- **D)** Bad Debts

> **Answer:** **B) Drawings**  
> **Explanation:** Drawings refers to any cash, goods, or assets withdrawn from the business by the owner for personal or household use. Drawings reduce the proprietor's total capital.

---

### Q8. Which of the following is an example of a **Fictitious Asset**?
- **A)** Patents & Trademarks
- **B)** Goodwill
- **C)** Deferred Revenue Expenditure (e.g., Preliminary Expenses, heavy advertising to be written off over years)
- **D)** Sundry Debtors

> **Answer:** **C) Deferred Revenue Expenditure (e.g., Preliminary Expenses, heavy advertising to be written off over years)**  
> **Explanation:** Fictitious assets are neither tangible assets nor do they carry realisable market value. They are unamortized deferred revenue expenditures or accumulated losses carried forward on the asset side of the balance sheet until written off against future profits. Goodwill and Patents are intangible real assets.

---

### Q9. How is a **Trade Discount** treated in the books of accounts?
- **A)** Debited to Discount Allowed Account
- **B)** Credited to Discount Received Account
- **C)** Deducted from the invoice value and not recorded separately in journal entries
- **D)** Shown as an asset on the balance sheet

> **Answer:** **C) Deducted from the invoice value and not recorded separately in journal entries**  
> **Explanation:** Trade discount is allowed by a seller on catalogue/list price to encourage bulk buying. It is deducted directly on the invoice. Only the net invoice amount is recorded in the books of accounts. Unlike Cash Discount, Trade Discount has no separate ledger account.

---

### Q10. A debtor who owes ₹20,000 to the business is declared insolvent and only 40% can be recovered. The unrecovered amount of ₹12,000 is classified as:
- **A)** Provision for Discount
- **B)** Bad Debts
- **C)** Trade Discount
- **D)** Capital Reserve

> **Answer:** **B) Bad Debts**  
> **Explanation:** Any amount legitimately owed by a customer/debtor that becomes irrecoverable due to insolvency, bankruptcy, or default is treated as an irrecoverable business loss known as a "Bad Debt" and debited to the Nominal account `Bad Debts A/c`.

---

### Q11. Expenditure incurred to install a newly purchased plant & machinery is:
- **A)** Revenue Expenditure (debited to Installation Charges A/c)
- **B)** Capital Expenditure (debited to Machinery A/c)
- **C)** Deferred Revenue Expenditure
- **D)** Operational Overhead (debited to Factory Rent)

> **Answer:** **B) Capital Expenditure (debited to Machinery A/c)**  
> **Explanation:** Any expenditure incurred to bring a fixed asset into working condition (such as freight, transit insurance, site preparation, and initial installation charges) increases the capacity/efficiency of the asset and is capitalized by adding it to the cost of that asset.

## Section 3: Accounting Concepts, Principles & Conventions (GAAP)

### Q12. The convention of treating the owner and business as distinct entities is known as the:
- **A)** Going Concern Assumption
- **B)** Business Entity (Separate Entity) Concept
- **C)** Money Measurement Concept
- **D)** Accrual Concept

> **Answer:** **B) Business Entity (Separate Entity) Concept**  
> **Explanation:** Under the Business Entity concept, the business enterprise is treated as a separate legal and financial entity distinct from its owners. It is because of this concept that capital contributed by the owner is treated as an internal liability for the enterprise.

---

### Q13. Valuation of closing stock at **"Cost price or Net Realizable Value (Market Value), whichever is lower"** is based on:
- **A)** Dual Aspect Concept
- **B)** Materiality Convention
- **C)** Prudence (Conservatism) Convention
- **D)** Historical Cost Concept

> **Answer:** **C) Prudence (Conservatism) Convention**  
> **Explanation:** The rule of Conservatism/Prudence states: "Do not anticipate profits, but provide for all foreseeable losses." If market value is lower than cost, the anticipated loss is recorded immediately by valuing stock at market value; if market value is higher, the unearned gain is ignored.

---

### Q14. Under the **Going Concern Assumption**, a business enterprise is assumed to:
- **A)** Be liquidated within the current operating cycle
- **B)** Continue its normal business operations for an indefinite and foreseeable future
- **C)** Sell all its fixed assets to pay off creditors at current market prices
- **D)** Cease operations whenever there is an accounting net loss

> **Answer:** **B) Continue its normal business operations for an indefinite and foreseeable future**  
> **Explanation:** The going concern assumption presumes that the enterprise has neither the intention nor the necessity to liquidate or curtail materially the scale of its operations. It provides the foundation for classifying assets into fixed/non-current and current.

---

### Q15. Recording revenue when goods are delivered or services are performed, rather than when cash is received, follows the:
- **A)** Cash Basis of Accounting
- **B)** Accrual Concept / Revenue Recognition (Realization) Concept
- **C)** Consistency Principle
- **D)** Materiality Principle

> **Answer:** **B) Accrual Concept / Revenue Recognition (Realization) Concept**  
> **Explanation:** The Accrual principle mandates that transactions are recorded in the accounting period in which they occur (when rights/obligations are established), regardless of when cash settlement is actually made. Revenue is realized when legal ownership passes to the buyer.

---

### Q16. The **Matching Concept** requires that:
- **A)** Debits in the journal must always equal credits
- **B)** Expenses incurred during a period must be matched against revenues earned in that same period
- **C)** The business assets must match the total liabilities of competitor firms
- **D)** Balance sheet totals of current year must equal previous year

> **Answer:** **B) Expenses incurred during a period must be matched against revenues earned in that same period**  
> **Explanation:** The Matching principle states that all expenses incurred to earn revenue during an accounting period must be recognized and deducted from those revenues in that period to compute true and fair net profit or loss.

---

### Q17. Minor items such as the purchase of a wastepaper basket or pencil sharpener are charged immediately to expenses rather than capitalized as fixed assets because of the:
- **A)** Full Disclosure Principle
- **B)** Materiality Convention
- **C)** Dual Aspect Concept
- **D)** Historical Cost Concept

> **Answer:** **B) Materiality Convention**  
> **Explanation:** The Materiality convention dictates that trivial and insignificant items whose detailed tracking would not influence the economic decisions of a reasonable user can be expensed immediately rather than depreciated over multiple years.

## Section 4: Accounting Equation & Source Documents

### Q18. If a firm purchases goods worth ₹50,000 on credit from Mohan, what is the effect on the Accounting Equation?
- **A)** Assets increase by ₹50,000; Capital increases by ₹50,000
- **B)** Assets increase by ₹50,000 (Stock); Liabilities increase by ₹50,000 (Creditors)
- **C)** Assets decrease by ₹50,000; Liabilities decrease by ₹50,000
- **D)** Only Assets increase, no change in liabilities

> **Answer:** **B) Assets increase by ₹50,000 (Stock); Liabilities increase by ₹50,000 (Creditors)**  
> **Explanation:** Goods purchased increase current assets (Inventory/Stock) by ₹50,000. Because they are purchased on credit, the liability to Creditor Mohan also increases by ₹50,000. Both sides increase by ₹50,000, maintaining equality.

---

### Q19. When goods are sold on credit, the source document issued by the seller to the buyer is:
- **A)** Cash Memo
- **B)** Sales Invoice / Tax Invoice
- **C)** Credit Note
- **D)** Pay-in-Slip

> **Answer:** **B) Sales Invoice / Tax Invoice**  
> **Explanation:** A Sales Invoice (or Bill) is prepared when goods are sold on credit. It contains details of items, quantity, rate, trade discount, and payment terms. Cash Memo is issued exclusively for cash sales.

---

### Q20. When a customer returns damaged goods to a supplier, the supplier acknowledges receipt and records the reduction in customer's balance by issuing a:
- **A)** Debit Note
- **B)** Credit Note
- **C)** Receipt Voucher
- **D)** Cheque Counterfoil

> **Answer:** **B) Credit Note**  
> **Explanation:** The supplier credits the customer's account (reducing the debtor balance) and issues a **Credit Note** to inform the customer that their account has been credited with the specified amount. (The customer initially sends a Debit Note).

---

### Q21. A business starts with Capital of ₹3,00,000 and Liabilities of ₹1,20,000. During the year, it earns a Profit of ₹50,000 and the owner withdraws ₹20,000 for personal use. What are the Total Assets at year-end?
- **A)** ₹4,20,000
- **B)** ₹4,50,000
- **C)** ₹4,70,000
- **D)** ₹3,70,000

> **Answer:** **B) ₹4,50,000**  
> **Explanation:**  
> 1. $\text{Closing Capital} = \text{Opening Capital} + \text{Profit} - \text{Drawings}$  
>    $\text{Closing Capital} = 3,00,000 + 50,000 - 20,000 = 3,30,000$.  
> 2. $\text{Total Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Closing Capital} = 1,20,000 + 3,30,000 = ₹4,50,000$.

## Section 5: Classification of Accounts & Rules of Debit & Credit

### Q22. "Outstanding Salary Account" and "Prepaid Insurance Account" are classified under traditional accounting as:
- **A)** Real Accounts
- **B)** Nominal Accounts
- **C)** Representative Personal Accounts
- **D)** Artificial Personal Accounts

> **Answer:** **C) Representative Personal Accounts**  
> **Explanation:** When an expense or income has a prefix or suffix (like Outstanding, Prepaid, Accrued, or Received in Advance), it ceases to be Nominal and represents specific persons/employees to whom payment is due or from whom service is receivable. Hence, it is a Representative Personal Account.

---

### Q23. Bank Account is categorized as which type of account?
- **A)** Real Account (because it contains cash)
- **B)** Personal Account (Artificial Legal Person)
- **C)** Nominal Account (operational expense)
- **D)** Fictitious Account

> **Answer:** **B) Personal Account (Artificial Legal Person)**  
> **Explanation:** A bank is an artificial legal person (a registered banking corporate entity). Therefore, in traditional accounting, a Bank Account is classified as a Personal Account (Rule: Debit the receiver, Credit the giver).

---

### Q24. What is the Golden Rule of Accounting for **Real Accounts**?
- **A)** Debit the receiver, Credit the giver
- **B)** Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out
- **C)** Debit all expenses and losses, Credit all incomes and gains
- **D)** Debit the debtor, Credit the creditor

> **Answer:** **B) Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out**  
> **Explanation:** Real accounts relate to properties, tangible assets, and intangible assets owned by the enterprise (Cash, Machinery, Building, Furniture). When an asset enters the business, it is debited; when it leaves the business, it is credited.

---

### Q25. Under the Modern (American) Classification, which two account categories increase with a **Debit (Dr.)**?
- **A)** Liabilities and Capital
- **B)** Assets and Expenses
- **C)** Capital and Revenues
- **D)** Liabilities and Revenues

> **Answer:** **B) Assets and Expenses**  
> **Explanation:** Under the DEAL framework:
> - **D**rawings / **E**xpenses / **A**ssets increase with a **Debit (Dr.)** and decrease with a **Credit (Cr.)**.
> - **C**apital / **L**iabilities / **R**evenue (CLEAR) increase with a **Credit (Cr.)** and decrease with a **Debit (Dr.)**.

---

### Q26. When goods costing ₹8,000 are distributed as **free samples** for product promotion, which account is credited?
- **A)** Advertisement Account
- **B)** Sales Account
- **C)** Purchases Account
- **D)** Free Samples Reserve Account

> **Answer:** **C) Purchases Account**  
> **Explanation:** Free sample distribution is an advertising expense. Because the goods are disbursed at cost price without generating sales revenue, `Purchases A/c` is credited to reduce the inventory balance at cost.  
> *Journal Entry:* `Advertisement A/c Dr. To Purchases A/c Cr.`

---

### Q27. Which of the following transactions will **not** affect the total of the Balance Sheet?
- **A)** Purchase of machinery on credit
- **B)** Cash collected from sundry debtors
- **C)** Additional capital introduced in cash by the owner
- **D)** Payment of bank loan through bank transfer

> **Answer:** **B) Cash collected from sundry debtors**  
> **Explanation:** When cash is collected from a debtor, Cash (Asset) increases while Debtors (Asset) decreases by the exact same amount. This is an asset-swap transaction that leaves total assets and total liabilities unchanged.

---

### Q28. Proprietary Capital is treated as an **internal liability** of the enterprise primarily because of:
- **A)** Conservatism Convention
- **B)** Business Entity Concept
- **C)** Accrual Principle
- **D)** Full Disclosure Principle

> **Answer:** **B) Business Entity Concept**  
> **Explanation:** Under the Business Entity principle, the business unit is distinct from its owner. Funds brought into the business by the proprietor are viewed as claims owed by the business back to the owner, making Capital an internal liability on the Balance Sheet.

---

### Q29. A customer paid ₹9,800 against a bill of ₹10,000 in full settlement. The ₹200 difference is:
- **A)** Cash Discount Allowed (Expense/Loss debited in seller's books)
- **B)** Trade Discount (not recorded in journal)
- **C)** Bad Debt
- **D)** Rebate on sales quality

> **Answer:** **A) Cash Discount Allowed (Expense/Loss debited in seller's books)**  
> **Explanation:** Prompt payment incentives granted at the time of settling cash debts are Cash Discounts. For the seller receiving cash, it is a loss/expense called `Discount Allowed A/c` (Debited). For the paying customer, it is `Discount Received A/c` (Credited).

---

### Q30. The primary purpose of preparing a **Trial Balance** is:
- **A)** To determine true net profit or loss of the period
- **B)** To verify the arithmetical accuracy of ledger postings and ensure debits equal credits
- **C)** To calculate income tax payable to the government
- **D)** To assess market valuation of company shares

> **Answer:** **B) To verify the arithmetical accuracy of ledger postings and ensure debits equal credits**  
> **Explanation:** A Trial Balance is a statement showing the debit and credit balances extracted from all ledger accounts on a given date. It verifies the arithmetical accuracy of double-entry postings before final accounts (Trading, P&L, Balance Sheet) are drafted.
