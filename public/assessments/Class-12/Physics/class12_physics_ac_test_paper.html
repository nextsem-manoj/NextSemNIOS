Section A • Core Conceptual Definitions & Physical Meaning 6 Questions × 1M = 6 Marks
Q1. Define Root Mean Square (RMS) Value of an Alternating Current. [1 Mark]
Why cannot an alternating current be measured using an ordinary moving-coil DC ammeter? Explain the thermal principle used.
Q2. Distinguish between Inductive Reactance (XL) and Capacitive Reactance (XC). [1 Mark]
How does each reactance vary with the frequency of the applied AC supply? Sketch their respective frequency variation trends.
Q3. Define Impedance (Z) of an LCR series circuit. [1 Mark]
State its SI unit and dimensional formula. Under what operating condition does impedance equal the pure ohmic resistance R?
Q4. What is meant by Wattless Current in an AC circuit? [1 Mark]
State the exact phase condition between voltage and current required for the current to be completely wattless.
Q5. Define Power Factor (cos φ) of an AC circuit. [1 Mark]
What is the significance of a low power factor during the long-distance transmission of electric power by power companies?
Q6. Define Sharpness of Resonance and Quality Factor (Q-factor). [1 Mark]
Write the expression for Q-factor in terms of L, C, and R. Why is a high Q-factor desirable in tuning circuits of radio receivers?

Section C (Part I) • Essential Mathematical Derivations 2 Questions × 4M = 8 Marks
Q7. Derivation of RMS Value of Alternating Current from Joule Heating. [4 Marks]
An alternating current is represented by i = im sin(ωt).
(a) Write the instantaneous power dissipated in a pure resistor R as a function of time.
(b) Integrate the instantaneous power over one complete cycle T to prove that average power P = ½ im² R.
(c) Equate this average power to equivalent DC heating I²R to establish Irms = im / √2 = 0.707 im.
Q8. AC Voltage Applied to a Pure Inductor: Current Phase Lag & Zero Power. [4 Marks]
An AC voltage v = vm sin(ωt) is applied across an ideal inductor of self-inductance L.
(a) Using Kirchhoff's loop rule v - L(di/dt) = 0, integrate to find the expression for current i(t). Show why the integration constant is zero.
(b) Prove that current lags the applied voltage by π/2 and define inductive reactance XL.
(c) Calculate the instantaneous power and rigorously prove that the average power absorbed over a complete cycle is zero.

Section C (Part II) • Advanced Circuit Derivations & Transformers 2 Questions × 5M = 10 Marks
Q9. Series LCR Circuit: Phasor Diagram Derivation for Impedance & Resonance. [5 Marks]
A series LCR circuit is connected to an AC source v = vm sin(ωt).
(a) Draw the phasor diagram representing the current phasor I and voltage phasors VR, VL, and VC.
(b) Using the Pythagorean relation for the voltage triangle, derive the analytical expression for impedance Z = √[R² + (XL - XC)²] and phase angle tan φ = (XL - XC)/R.
(c) Deduce the condition for electrical resonance, find the resonant frequency ω0, and state why resonance cannot occur in an RL or RC circuit.
Q10. Principle, Working & Theory of a Transformer; Energy Losses. [5 Marks]
(a) State the basic operating principle of an electrical transformer.
(b) Using Faraday's law of induction, derive the transformation ratio Vs/Vp = Ns/Np = Ip/Is for an ideal 100% efficient transformer.
(c) Distinguish between a step-up and step-down transformer in terms of turns ratio and current.
(d) Explain two major causes of energy loss in practical transformers (e.g., eddy currents, flux leakage) and explain how each loss is minimised.
Section D • Standard NCERT Numerical Problems (With Full Working Steps) 4 Questions = 20 Marks
Q11. AC Mains Supply & Pure Inductive / Capacitive Circuits (NCERT 7.1, 7.2 & 7.4) [4 Marks]
(a) A light bulb is rated at 100 W for a 220 V, 50 Hz supply. Calculate: (i) the resistance of the bulb, (ii) the peak voltage of the source, and (iii) the rms current flowing through the bulb.
(b) A pure inductor of 25.0 mH is connected across a 220 V, 50 Hz source. Find the inductive reactance XL and the rms current in the circuit.
Q12. Pure Capacitor & Frequency Doubling Effect (NCERT 7.4) [4 Marks]
A 15.0 μF capacitor is connected to a 220 V, 50 Hz AC source.
(a) Calculate the capacitive reactance XC and the rms current I in the circuit.
(b) Find the peak current im.
(c) If the frequency of the AC source is doubled (from 50 Hz to 100 Hz), determine what happens to the capacitive reactance and the circuit current.
Q13. Comprehensive Series LCR Circuit Analysis (NCERT Example 7.8) [6 Marks]
A sinusoidal voltage of peak value vm = 283 V and frequency 50 Hz is applied to a series LCR circuit in which R = 3 Ω, L = 25.48 mH, and C = 796 μF. Calculate:
(a) Inductive reactance XL and capacitive reactance XC.
(b) The total impedance Z of the circuit.
(c) The phase difference φ between the source voltage and current. Does current lead or lag?
(d) The power factor cos φ and the total average power dissipated in the circuit.
Q14. Resonant Frequency & Transformer Calculation (NCERT Example 7.9 & Section 7.8) [6 Marks]
(a) For the series LCR circuit in Q13, calculate the resonant angular frequency ω0 and linear resonant frequency νr. Calculate the impedance, rms current, and power dissipated at resonance.
(b) An ideal step-up transformer has 100 turns in its primary winding and 200 turns in its secondary winding. If a 220 V AC supply at 10 A is connected across the primary, calculate the secondary voltage Vs and secondary current Is.
