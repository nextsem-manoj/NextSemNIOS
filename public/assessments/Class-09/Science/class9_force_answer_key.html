Section B • Force Measurement & Action-Reaction Pairs (Solutions) 6 Marks (2 × 3M)
9. Spring Balance Working Principle & Reading Solution: Total: 3M
(a) Device Name: Spring Balance (Dynamometer). [1M]
(b) Scientific Principle: Operates on the principle of spring extension (Hooke's Law): the stretch (elongation) produced in a coiled spring is directly proportional to the magnitude of the gravitational/pulling force applied to it (F ∝ x). [1M]
(c) Force Calculation:
Given: m = 400 g = 0.4 kg,   g = 10 m/s2.
Applied Force F = m × g = 0.4 kg × 10 m/s2 = 4.0 N. (Pointer registers 4 N on calibrated scale). [1M]
10. Friction Definition & Action-Reaction Pairs Solution: Total: 3M
(a) Definition of Friction & Rolling Ball: The force of friction is a contact resistive force that acts parallel to the contacting surfaces and opposes the relative motion between them. A rolling football stops because the force of friction between the grass and the ball continuously exerts an unbalanced opposing force, bringing its velocity to zero. [1.5M]
(b) Two Everyday Action-Reaction Force Pairs: [1.5M]
  • Pair 1 (Swimmer in water): Swimmer's hands push water backward (Action), and water pushes swimmer forward (Reaction).
  • Pair 2 (Firing a rifle): Gun exerts forward force on bullet (Action), bullet exerts equal backward recoil force on gun (Reaction).
Section C • Foundational Formula Calculations (F = ma Solutions) 6 Marks (2 × 3M)
11. Force & Acceleration Solution
Step 1: Given: F1 = 18 N, m = 3 kg
Formula: a1 = F1 / m
a1 = 18 / 3 = 6 m/s2 [1.5M]
Step 2: When force is increased:
F2 = 30 N, m = 3 kg
a2 = F2 / m = 30 / 3 = 10 m/s2 [1.5M]
12. Braking Force Solution
Step 1: Acceleration (Retardation):
Given: m = 200 kg, u = 20 m/s, v = 0 m/s, t = 4 s
a = (v − u) / t = (0 − 20) / 4 = −5 m/s2
(Retardation magnitude = 5 m/s2) [1.5M]
Step 2: Braking Force:
F = m × a = 200 kg × (−5 m/s2) = −1000 N
Braking force magnitude = 1000 N (acts opposite to direction of travel). [1.5M]

Section D • Newton's First Law & Inertia (Solutions) 6 Marks (2 × 3M)
13. Newton's First Law & Inertia Solution: Total: 3M
(a) Statement: An object remains in a state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line unless compelled to change that state by an applied external unbalanced force. [1M]
(b) Direct Measure of Inertia: Mass is the quantitative measure of the inertia of a body. [1M]
(c) Cricket Ball vs Tennis Ball: A cricket ball has greater inertia because it has significantly greater mass than a tennis ball of the same size. Since inertia is directly proportional to mass (Inertia ∝ m), a heavier body resists change in state more strongly. [1M]
14. Scientific Reasons Based on Everyday Inertia: Total: 3M
(a) Leaning sideways on curve: Due to inertia of direction. The passenger's body tends to continue moving in its original straight-line path while the car's wheels turn inward. [1M]
(b) Beating a carpet for dust: Due to inertia of rest. When beaten, the carpet suddenly moves with the stick, while the dust particles stay in their position of rest and fall off under gravity. [1M]
(c) Tying luggage on bus roof: When the bus suddenly accelerates, stops, or turns, loose luggage tends to slide off or fall forward/backward due to inertia of rest / motion / direction. Tying prevents relative motion. [1M]
Section E • Student Graph Drawing Workshop (Model Graphs & Key Properties) 10 Marks (4 × 2.5M)
15. Position–Time (s–t): UNIFORM MOTION 2.5M
Slope = Speed (or Velocity) (v = Δs / Δt). Constant slope = constant speed.
16. Position–Time (s–t): NON-UNIFORM MOTION 2.5M
Shape = Curved line (Parabolic). Slope increases with time (Accelerating).
17. Velocity–Time (v–t): UNIFORM VELOCITY 2.5M
Area under v–t curve gives = Displacement / Distance (s = v × t).
18. Velocity–Time (v–t): UNIFORM ACCELERATION 2.5M
Slope of this v–t curve gives = Acceleration (a = Δv / Δt).
