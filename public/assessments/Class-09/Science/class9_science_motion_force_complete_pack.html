Section C Theoretical Explanations & Newton's First Law Rubric
5 Marks [2 × 2.5]
Award 1.5 marks for scientific principle + 1.0 mark for logical real-world application.
1. Bus Passengers Falling Forward: 2.5 Marks
Model Answer: Initially the passenger's body is in forward motion with the bus. When brakes are applied, the lower body in contact with the bus floor comes to rest immediately, but the upper body continues in its forward state of motion due to Inertia of Motion (Newton's First Law). Hence, passengers fall forward.
2. Mass as the Quantitative Measure of Inertia: 2.5 Marks
Model Answer: Inertia is an object's resistance to change its state of rest or motion. Greater the mass of a body, greater is the resistance offered. For example, kicking a football easily sets it in motion, whereas kicking a stone of the same size requires huge force and barely moves because the stone has much greater mass and hence greater inertia.

Section F Applied Dynamics Real-World Rubric
5 Marks [2 × 2.5]
Scoring Rubric: 1.5 marks for momentum-time relationship (F ∝ Δp / Δt) + 1.0 mark for impact consequence.
1. Fielder Pulling Hands Backward: 2.5 Marks
Model Answer: According to Newton's Second Law, F = Δp / Δt. When the fielder pulls his hands backward, he increases the time interval (Δt) taken to stop the ball's momentum. Since force is inversely proportional to time, a larger time interval significantly reduces the impact force (F) on the hands, preventing pain or injury.
2. Falling on Cemented Floor vs Sand Heap: 2.5 Marks
Model Answer: On a rigid cement floor, the body comes to rest almost instantaneously (Δt → 0), producing a very high rate of change of momentum and exerting a massive impact force that causes severe injuries. On loose sand, the sand yields and increases the stopping time (Δt), drastically reducing the impact force.
