### **Q1.** Which one is the advantage of accounting?
- (a) Replacement of Memory
- (b) Shows the present value of business
- (c) Accounting does not record price level changes.
- (d) Accounting is not fully exact.

> 💡 **Strategic Hint:** Differentiate between *benefits* (what helps business owners overcome human memory limits) versus *limitations* (like historical cost bias and lack of exactness).
> 
> 🎯 **Step-by-Step Solution & Answer:**  
> - Human memory cannot store thousands of daily commercial transactions. Accounting systematically logs every transaction in books of prime entry.

### **Q21.** Give the rules of debit and credit of the three types of accounts. (3 Marks)

> 💡 **Strategic Hint:** Name the three traditional accounts (Personal, Real, Nominal) and state their golden rules with one quick example each.

🎯 **Complete Solution:**
1. **Personal Accounts:** (Relate to individuals, firms, companies)  
   👉 **Rule:** *Debit the Receiver, Credit the Giver.*  
   *(e.g., Paid cash to Suresh: Suresh A/c Dr. to Cash A/c).*
2. **Real Accounts:** (Relate to tangible/intangible properties and assets)  
   👉 **Rule:** *Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out.*  
   *(e.g., Purchased Furniture: Furniture A/c Dr. to Cash A/c).*
3. **Nominal Accounts:** (Relate to expenses, losses, incomes, gains)  
   👉 **Rule:** *Debit all expenses and losses, Credit all incomes and gains.*  
   *(e.g., Paid Salary: Salary A/c Dr. to Cash A/c; Received Commission: Cash A/c Dr. to Commission A/c).*

### **Q22.** Explain the meaning of any three of the following terms: (i) Assets (ii) Debtors (iii) Goods (iv) Capital (3 Marks)

> 💡 **Strategic Hint:** Provide the formal accounting definition, economic purpose, and a concrete business example for any three.

🎯 **Complete Solution (Covering all 4):**
- **(i) Assets:** Economic resources owned by a business resulting from past transactions from which future economic benefits are expected to flow (e.g. Cash, Machinery, Inventory).
- **(ii) Debtors:** Persons or customer firms who have purchased goods/services on credit and owe money to the business.
- **(iii) Goods:** Physical products or commodities in which the business enterprise regularly deals/trades for the purpose of resale or manufacturing.
- **(iv) Capital:** The financial resources and assets contributed by the proprietor/owner to start and operate the business ($Capital = Assets - Liabilities$).

### **Q23.** Explain briefly any three advantages of accounting. OR Explain any three limitations of Accounting. (3 Marks)

🎯 **Complete Solution:**
- **Advantages (Any Three):**
  1. **Systematic Financial Record:** Replaces human memory by recording all transactions systematically.
  2. **Ascertainment of Profit or Loss:** Enables the preparation of the Trading & Profit and Loss Account.
  3. **Evidence in Legal Matters:** Systematically maintained books serve as documentary proof in courts of law.
- **OR Limitations (Any Three):**
  1. **Historical Cost Bias:** Records assets at historical cost, ignoring inflation and price level changes.
  2. **Ignores Qualitative Elements:** Non-monetary factors like staff efficiency and management quality are excluded.
  3. **Affected by Window Dressing:** Financial statements can be manipulated to present a more favorable picture.

### **Q30.** Explain the qualitative characteristics of accounting information. OR What is accounting? Define its objectives (any five). (6 Marks)

🎯 **Complete Solution:**
1. **Reliability:** Accounting data must be verifiable, factual, free from bias, and supported by source documents.
2. **Relevance:** Information must be capable of making a difference in economic decisions by helping users evaluate past, present, or future events.
3. **Understandability:** Information must be presented in a clear, concise format intelligible to users with reasonable business knowledge.
4. **Comparability:** Allows intra-firm and inter-firm comparisons over time using consistent accounting principles.

### **Q31.** Explain the following accounting principles/concepts: (6 Marks)
(i) Accrual Concept | (ii) Money Measurement Concept | (iii) Conservatism / Prudence Concept

🎯 **Complete Solution:**
- **(i) Accrual Concept:** Transactions are recognized in the period in which they occur/earn/incur, regardless of whether cash is received or paid.
- **(ii) Money Measurement Concept:** Only transactions and events that can be expressed in terms of money are recorded in the accounting books.
- **(iii) Conservatism / Prudence Concept:** Anticipate no profits, but provide for all possible losses. (e.g., valuing closing stock at cost or net realizable value, whichever is lower).
