### 4.1 Cash Book (रोकड़ बही)
A special journal used to record all **cash receipts** (Debited on left side) and **cash payments** (Credited on right side) chronologically. It acts as both a **Journal** (original book of entry) and a **Ledger** (no separate cash account needed).

---

#### 4.1.1 Single Column Cash Book (सरल रोकड़ बही)
* **Concept:** Contains only one money column on each side (Receipts on Debit, Payments on Credit). Records only cash transactions.
* **Example:**
  * Nov 01: Commenced business with Cash: ₹30,000 *(Debit: To Capital A/c ₹30,000)*
  * Nov 08: Paid Insurance: ₹6,000 *(Credit: By Insurance A/c ₹6,000)*
  * Nov 16: Cash Sales: ₹28,000 *(Debit: To Sales A/c ₹28,000)*
  * **Closing Cash Balance (c/d):** Total Receipts (₹58,000) − Payments (₹6,000) = **₹52,000**.

---

#### 4.1.2 Double Column Cash Book (द्वि-स्तंभीय रोकड़ बही)
* **Concept:** Has two money columns on each side — **Cash** and **Bank**.
* **Contra Entry (प्रति प्रविष्टि - 'C'):** Any transaction involving both Cash and Bank simultaneously (e.g., cash deposited into bank or cash withdrawn from bank for office use). It is recorded on **both** sides of the cash book and marked with **'C'** in the Ledger Folio (L.F.) column. No posting is required to the ledger.
* **Example:**
  * *Deposited cash ₹10,000 into bank:*
    * **Debit side (Receipts):** Bank column ₹10,000 *(To Cash A/c, L.F. = 'C')*
    * **Credit side (Payments):** Cash column ₹10,000 *(By Bank A/c, L.F. = 'C')*
  * *Withdrew ₹4,000 from bank for office use:*
    * **Debit side:** Cash column ₹4,000 *(To Bank A/c, L.F. = 'C')*
    * **Credit side:** Bank column ₹4,000 *(By Cash A/c, L.F. = 'C')*

---

#### 4.1.3 Petty Cash Book (खुदरा रोकड़ बही) & Imprest System
* **Concept:** Used by a petty cashier to record repetitive minor payments (cartage, tea/refreshment, postage, bus fare, stationery).
* **Imprest System (अग्रदाय प्रणाली):** Head Cashier advances a fixed sum (e.g., ₹2,000) at the start of a period. At period end, the exact amount spent (e.g., ₹1,780) is reimbursed so the petty cashier begins the next period with the full original advance (₹2,000).
* **Example:**
  * Imprest advance: ₹2,000
  * Expenses: Cartage ₹50 + Bus fare ₹20 + Postage ₹30 + Refreshment ₹80 = ₹180
  * Balance in Hand = ₹2,000 − ₹180 = **₹1,820**
  * Head cashier reimburses **₹180** to restore imprest to ₹2,000.

---

#### 4.1.4 Balancing of Cash Book (रोकड़ बही का संतुलन)
* **Rule:** Cash column **always** shows a **Debit balance** (or zero) because a business cannot pay more cash than it possesses.
* **Bank Column:** Can have either a **Debit balance** (favourable deposit) or a **Credit balance** (Bank Overdraft).
* **Source Documents:** Duplicate receipt copies for receipts, and signed payment vouchers/bills for payments.

---

### 4.2 Purchases (Journal) Book (क्रय बही)
* **Rule:** Records **ONLY CREDIT purchases of goods/merchandise** dealt in. Cash purchases (go to Cash Book) and credit purchase of assets (go to Journal Proper) are **NEVER** entered here.
* **Example:**
  * *Bought 40 Registers @ ₹60 each from Rahul Traders (Invoice No. 20041) less 10% Trade Discount:*
  * Gross = ₹2,400 − 10% Trade Discount (₹240) = **₹2,160** entered in Purchases Book.

---

### 4.3 Purchases Return (Journal) Book (क्रय वापसी बही)
* **Rule:** Records goods purchased on credit that are returned to suppliers (due to defects, wrong specifications, transit damage).
* **Source Document:** **Debit Note (डेबिट नोट)** issued to the supplier intimating debit to their account.
* **Example:** Returned 10 defective registers to Rahul Traders (List Price ₹600 less 10% Trade Discount) = **₹540**.

---

### 4.4 Sales (Journal) Book (विक्रय बही)
* **Rule:** Records **ONLY CREDIT sales of goods/merchandise**. Cash sales and sale of used office equipment/furniture are excluded.
* **Example:**
  * *Sold 20 Pocket Radios @ ₹70 each to Amit Traders (Bill No. 4321):*
  * Net amount entered = 20 × ₹70 = **₹1,400**.

---

### 4.5 Sales Return (Journal) Book (विक्रय वापसी बही)
* **Rule:** Records goods sold on credit returned by customers (Return Inwards).
* **Source Document:** **Credit Note (क्रेडिट नोट)** sent to the customer intimating credit given to their account.
* **Example:** Amit Traders returns 2 radios @ ₹70 = **₹140** entered in Sales Return Book with Credit Note issued.

---

### 4.6 Journal Proper (मुख्य रोज़नामचा)
* **Concept:** The residual book of original entry for transactions that do not fit into any of the 6 special subsidiary books:
  1. **Opening Entries (प्रारंभिक प्रविष्टियां):** Bringing forward previous year's assets, liabilities, and capital.
  2. **Closing Entries (अंतिम प्रविष्टियां):** Transferring nominal accounts to Trading and Profit & Loss A/c.
  3. **Rectification Entries (अशुद्धि शोधन):** Correcting accounting errors.
  4. **Adjustment Entries (समायोजन प्रविष्टियां):** Outstanding expenses, prepaid expenses, accrued income, depreciation.
  5. **Credit purchase or sale of Capital Assets:** Buying office furniture or machinery on credit.
* **Example:** *Purchased office computer on credit from HP Ltd for ₹45,000:*
  * `Office Equipment A/c  Dr.  45,000`
  * `   To HP Ltd A/c             45,000`

---

### 4.7 Balancing the Accounts (खातों का संतुलन निकालना)
* **Process:** Compute the sum of the debit side and credit side. Insert the difference as `Balance c/d` on the smaller side to equalize totals, and bring it down to the opposite side below the total line as `Balance b/d`.
  * If Debit Total > Credit Total $\rightarrow$ **Debit Balance** (Assets, Expenses, Losses).
  * If Credit Total > Debit Total $\rightarrow$ **Credit Balance** (Liabilities, Capital, Incomes, Gains).

### Page 54: Short Answer Questions

1. **How is the cash book both a journal and a ledger?**
   * **Answer:** It is a **journal** because all cash/bank transactions are recorded directly in it for the first time in chronological order from source documents. It is a **ledger** because it is maintained in T-account ledger format (Dr./Cr.), is balanced at period-end, and completely replaces the Cash Account in the ledger.
2. **What is the purpose of a contra entry?**
   * **Answer:** To record transactions that affect both Cash and Bank simultaneously (cash deposit or cash withdrawal for office). Since both sides appear in the same book, contra entries eliminate redundant posting to the ledger.
3. **What are special purpose books?**
   * **Answer:** Subsidiary journals dedicated to recording specific repetitive transactions (Cash Book, Purchases Book, Sales Book, Purchases Return Book, Sales Return Book, and Journal Proper), enabling division of labour and quick retrieval.
4. **What is a petty cash book? How is it prepared?**
   * **Answer:** A subsidiary book recording high-frequency, low-value disbursements. Prepared using columnar/analytical format where payments are classified into specific expense columns (cartage, postage, conveyance, tea/refreshment, stationery, misc.).
5. **Explain the meaning of posting of journal entries.**
   * **Answer:** Transferring the recorded debit and credit aspects from books of original entry into their respective accounts in the ledger.
6. **Define the purpose of maintaining subsidiary journals.**
   * **Answer:** Division of labour, prevention of bulky single journals, specialized recording, internal audit checks, and elimination of ledger clutter.
7. **Write the difference between Return Inwards and Return Outwards.**
   * **Return Inwards (Sales Return):** Goods returned by credit customers to the business; source document is Credit Note.
   * **Return Outwards (Purchases Return):** Goods returned by the business to suppliers; source document is Debit Note.
8. **What do you understand by Ledger Folio (L.F.)?**
   * **Answer:** The column indicating the page number in the ledger where the corresponding debit or credit entry has been posted.
9. **Difference between Trade Discount and Cash Discount:**
   * **Trade Discount:** Allowed on catalogue list price for bulk purchases; **not recorded** in books of account.
   * **Cash Discount:** Allowed for prompt or early cash payment within a specified period; **recorded** in the cash book/journal.
10. **Process of preparing ledger from a journal:**
    * Locate the ledger account $\rightarrow$ Enter date $\rightarrow$ Write contra account title in Particulars prefixed with 'To' (Dr.) or 'By' (Cr.) $\rightarrow$ Enter Journal Folio $\rightarrow$ Enter amount.
11. **What is Imprest amount in petty cash book?**
    * **Answer:** A designated fixed advance given to the petty cashier at the start of a period, which is replenished at the end by the exact total spent.

### Pages 54–55: Long Answer Questions

1. **Need for Special Purpose Books:** In growing enterprises, recording hundreds of daily transactions in a single general journal causes clerical bottlenecks, errors, and unmanageable ledgers. Special purpose books divide labour, group similar transactions, and facilitate rapid ledger posting.
2. **Types of Cash Book:**
   * *Single Column Cash Book:* For businesses transacting exclusively in cash.
   * *Double Column Cash Book:* Maintains simultaneous Cash and Bank columns with contra tracking.
   * *Petty Cash Book:* Handles minor repetitive out-of-pocket expenses under the imprest system.
3. **Contra Entry Treatment in Double Column Cash Book:**
   * Cash deposited into Bank: Debit Bank column (`To Cash A/c`), Credit Cash column (`By Bank A/c`). Write 'C' in L.F. on both sides.
   * Cash withdrawn for office: Debit Cash column (`To Bank A/c`), Credit Bank column (`By Cash A/c`). Write 'C' in L.F. on both sides.
4. **Advantages of Petty Cash Book:** Saves chief cashier's time, enforces strict control on pocket expenses, prevents ledger clutter, minimizes embezzlement risks.
5. **Advantages of Sub-dividing Journal:** Facilitates division of clerical work, specialized recording, prompt reference, efficient internal check, and automated posting of periodic totals.
6. **Balancing of Accounts:** Tallying both sides of a ledger account periodically to find the net financial standing (debit surplus = asset/expense; credit surplus = liability/income).

## 🧮 Detailed Working Notes & Calculations (कार्यशील टिप्पणियाँ)

### 📌 Fundamental Rule of Accounting (लेखांकन का मूल नियम):
> **Trade Discount (व्यापारिक छूट):** Trade discount is deducted directly from the List Price/Catalogue Price to arrive at the actual transaction value. **It is never recorded in the books of accounts as an expense or income.**

---

#### 1. Transaction (i):
* **List Price:** ₹20,000
* **Less:** Trade Discount @ 20% = $\text{₹}20,000 \times \frac{20}{100} = \text{₹}4,000$
* **Net Purchase Value:** $\text{₹}20,000 - \text{₹}4,000 = \mathbf{\text{₹}16,000}$
* *Payment made via cheque $\rightarrow$ Credit Bank A/c.*

#### 2. Transaction (ii):
* **List Price:** ₹20,000
* **Less:** Trade Discount @ 15% = $\text{₹}20,000 \times \frac{15}{100} = \text{₹}3,000$
* **Net Cash Paid:** $\text{₹}20,000 - \text{₹}3,000 = \mathbf{\text{₹}17,000}$
* *Payment made in cash $\rightarrow$ Credit Cash A/c.*

#### 3. Transaction (iii):
* **List Price:** ₹30,000
* **Less:** Trade Discount @ 20% = $\text{₹}30,000 \times \frac{20}{100} = \text{₹}6,000$
* **Net Credit Purchase from Amrit:** $\text{₹}30,000 - \text{₹}6,000 = \mathbf{\text{₹}24,000}$
* *Since cash/cheque is not mentioned, it is a credit purchase $\rightarrow$ Credit Amrit.*

#### 4. Transaction (iv):
* **List Price:** ₹40,000
* **Actual Purchase Price:** $\mathbf{\text{₹}35,000}$ against cash.
* *The reduction of ₹5,000 is trade discount, so only the net amount of ₹35,000 is recorded.*

#### 5. Transaction (v):
* **List Price of Returned Goods:** ₹10,000
* **Less:** Trade Discount originally allowed @ 20% = $\text{₹}10,000 \times \frac{20}{100} = \text{₹}2,000$
* **Net Purchases Return Value:** $\text{₹}10,000 - \text{₹}2,000 = \mathbf{\text{₹}8,000}$
* *(खरीदते समय मिली 20% व्यापारिक छूट वापसी के समय भी सूची मूल्य से घटाई जाएगी।)*

#### 6. Transaction (vi):
* **List Price:** ₹40,000
* **Less:** Trade Discount @ 10% = $\text{₹}40,000 \times \frac{10}{100} = \text{₹}4,000$
* **Net Amount Received by Cheque:** $\text{₹}40,000 - \text{₹}4,000 = \mathbf{\text{₹}36,000}$

#### 7. Transaction (vii):
* **List Price:** ₹30,000
* **Less:** Trade Discount @ 10% = $\text{₹}30,000 \times \frac{10}{100} = \text{₹}3,000$
* **Net Cash Received:** $\text{₹}30,000 - \text{₹}3,000 = \mathbf{\text{₹}27,000}$

#### 8. Transaction (viii):
* **List Price:** ₹20,000
* **Less:** Trade Discount @ 10% = $\text{₹}20,000 \times \frac{10}{100} = \text{₹}2,000$
* **Net Credit Sales to Pawan:** $\text{₹}20,000 - \text{₹}2,000 = \mathbf{\text{₹}18,000}$

#### 9. Transaction (ix):
* **Agreed Selling Price:** $\mathbf{\text{₹}23,000}$ on credit.
* *(सूची मूल्य ₹25,000 का माल ₹23,000 में बेचा गया, अतः बिक्री ₹23,000 दर्ज होगी।)*

#### 10. Transaction (x):
* **Cost Price (लागत मूल्य):** ₹10,000
* **Add:** Profit @ 20% on Cost = $\text{₹}10,000 \times \frac{20}{100} = \text{₹}2,000$
* **Gross Selling Price (सूची मूल्य):** $\text{₹}10,000 + \text{₹}2,000 = \text{₹}12,000$
* **Less:** Trade Discount @ 10% on ₹12,000 = $\text{₹}12,000 \times \frac{10}{100} = \text{₹}1,200$
* **Net Selling Price:** $\text{₹}12,000 - \text{₹}1,200 = \mathbf{\text{₹}10,800}$

#### 11. Transaction (xi):
* **Base List Price:** ₹50,000
* **Add:** Profit @ 25% = $\text{₹}50,000 \times \frac{25}{100} = \text{₹}12,500$
* **Price before discount:** $\text{₹}50,000 + \text{₹}12,500 = \text{₹}62,500$
* **Less:** Trade Discount @ 10% on ₹62,500 = $\text{₹}62,500 \times \frac{10}{100} = \text{₹}6,250$
* **Net Cheque Received:** $\text{₹}62,500 - \text{₹}6,250 = \mathbf{\text{₹}56,250}$

#### 12. Transaction (xii):
* **List Price of Goods Returned by Aman:** ₹10,000
* **Less:** 10% Trade Discount (as sold in transaction vii) = $\text{₹}10,000 \times \frac{10}{100} = \text{₹}1,000$
* **Net Sales Return Value:** $\text{₹}10,000 - \text{₹}1,000 = \mathbf{\text{₹}9,000}$
