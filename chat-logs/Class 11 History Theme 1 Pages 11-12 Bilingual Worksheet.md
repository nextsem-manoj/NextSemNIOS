### 📚 Detailed Pedagogical Content Covered from Pages 11 & 12

| Section | NCERT Page | Core Concept & Content Breakdown |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Geography & Silt Deposition** | **Page 11** | The south is a desert where the first cities and writing emerged. Euphrates and Tigris rise in northern mountains, carrying silt (fine mud) deposited during floods. |
| **Natural Irrigation Channels** | **Page 11** | Euphrates overflow channels functioned as irrigation canals for wheat, barley, peas, and lentils, making it the most productive ancient agricultural system (surpassing the Roman Empire) despite insufficient rainfall. |
| **Steppe Pastoral Economy** | **Page 11** | Sheep and goats grazed on the steppe and mountain slopes (tracts too high to flood), yielding abundant milk, meat, and wool, supplemented by river fish and date-palms. |
| **The Significance of Urbanism** | **Page 12** | Cities are not mere population clusters. An urban economy becomes viable when it expands into non-food spheres: **manufactures, trade, and services**, ending individual self-sufficiency. |
| **Division of Labour** | **Page 12** | *"The division of labour is a mark of urban life."* Illustrated with the cylinder seal carver (requires bronze tools and colored stones), the toolmaker, and the charcoal supplier. |
| **Social Organisation & Records** | **Page 12** | Raw materials (fuel, metal, stones, wood) came from varied distant sources; organized storage, transport, command structures, and written administrative records were essential. |
| **The Warka Head Spotlight** | **Page 12 Box** | Sculpted in white marble at Uruk (<3000 BCE). Features delicate modeling of the woman's mouth, chin, and cheeks; eye inlays of **lapis lazuli** (blue), **shell** (white), and **bitumen** (black). |
| **Bronze Age Technology** | **Page 12** | Early cities date to c. 3000 BCE. Bronze is an alloy of **copper and tin** sourced from great distances. Essential for carpentry, bead drilling, stone seal carving, shell inlay cutting, and weapons. |
